## 1 Adjectives ending in -ed and -ing

### 1.1 Adjectives ending in -ed
Adjectives ending in -ed have the same form as the past participle of a verb, e.g. amazed, relieved, terrified, thrilled. We often use them to describe people’s feelings. They normally follow be, feel, seem, look, etc.

Example:

> I was amazed to hear I'd won the prize.

> What’s the matter? You seem a bit annoyed.

### 1.2 Adjectives ending in -ing
We use these adjectives to describe the effect that experiences or events have on us. They can be used before the noun or after be and other linking verbs.

Example:

> That was a terrifying experience.

> The explanation was rather confusing.

## 2 Adverbs

### 2.1 Formation of adverbs from adjectives

1. Many adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to the adjective form of the word, e.g. *clear* → *clearly*.

   - For adjectives ending in **-y**, drop the **y** and add **-ily**, e.g. *happy* → *happily*.
   - For adjectives ending in **-le**, drop the **e** and add **-ly**, e.g. *gentle* → *gently*.
   - For adjectives ending in **-ic**, add **-ally**, e.g. *automatic* → *automatically*.

2. Some words ending in **-ly** are adjectives only, not adverbs (e.g. *cowardly*, *friendly*). If an adverb is needed, a phrase must be used.

   Example: They greeted us in a friendly way/manner.

3. Some words ending in **-ly** can be used both as adjectives and adverbs, e.g. *hourly*, *daily*, *nightly*.

   Example: Take the medicine twice daily.

4. Some adverbs have the same form as adjectives, e.g. *early, fast, hard, straight*.

   Example: He’s got a fast (adj.) car and he drives it fast (adv.).

5. Some adverbs have two forms, one like the adjective and the other form ending in -ly.

   Examples: *free* / *freely*, *high* / *highly*.

**Usage examples from the page:**

- They greeted us in a friendly way/manner.
- Take the medicine twice daily.
- He has straight (adj.) hair. He looked straight (adv.) at me.
- The balloon was high up in the sky.

### 2.2 Comparison of adverbs
The comparison of adverbs is similar to that of adjectives. See section 4.5.

### 2.3 Adverbs of manner
These adverbs are used to say how the action of the verb is carried out.

Common adverbs of manner include: accurately, badly, carefully, patiently, straight, well.
